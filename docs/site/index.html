Mining that trains real AI models

Instead of burning electricity on hashes nobody uses, your computer trains small, checkable pieces of AI models, and earns VIBE for every piece that checks out.

    Start mining

    1. Paste the install line

      Into PowerShell on Windows, or Terminal on macOS and Linux. It needs Python 3.10 or newer; on Windows it installs Python for you if it's missing.

    2. Answer four questions

      Network
      Press Enter to join the public network.
      Wallet
      Choose a password. Setup then shows 24 recovery words, once: write them down. They're the only way back into your wallet if you lose its file or forget the password.
      Graphics card
      If it finds one, it offers to install GPU support, a download of up to 3 GB. Without one, training runs on your processor.
      Stake
      Nothing to do: your first 5 VIBE of pay becomes your stake.

    3. Start mining

      Say yes when setup asks. Ctrl+C stops it, and python -m vibehash worker starts it again any time. If your computer calls Python something else, the installer tells you what to type instead.

    Mining the DeepSeek job

    It takes an NVIDIA graphics card with 8 GB or more (RTX 20-series or newer), 16 GB of memory (32 GB is faster) and about 40 GB free on an SSD. Your PC downloads about 30 GB of the model, after asking. You choose where it goes and how much space it may use, and it's deleted when the job ends, unless you'd rather keep it for next time. No PC ever holds the whole 510 GB model. Other graphics cards and processors keep earning on the network's other jobs.

    python -m vibehash a menu of all of this: pick with the arrow keys

    python -m vibehash balance what you've earned

    python -m vibehash devices what this computer can train on

    python -m vibehash setup change the network, wallet or GPU support

    How it works

    1

    Get a piece of work

    Each round, about every 40 seconds, the network hands your computer a few training steps of a model someone has paid to have trained.

    2

    Train it

    On your graphics card if you have one, or your processor if not. An old laptop and a gaming rig earn the same rate per unit of work.

    3

    Get checked, get paid

    Other miners redo random bits of your work. If one disagrees, the network narrows it down to a single calculation and redoes just that. Work that checks out is paid by how much you did; cheaters lose their stake.

    What's live, and what's next

    • Live: fine-tuning open models. Teach a model like Qwen your own text. The network trains an add-on for it and you download the result. The node that runs the network never runs the model.
    • Live: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, in pieces. The first job teaches it to think shorter. Its 510 GB are cut so that no miner holds more than about 30 GB: the bottom layers are prepared once, in pieces anyone can run, and miners train the top four.
    • Next: the full set. The first job is a 1,500-problem pilot. The full 7,473 come next, reusing the pilot's prepared pieces.
    • Next: nobody in charge. Today one node runs the network and one storage bucket holds the hand-offs. More validators, and miners hosting the hand-offs, take that away.

    Questions people ask

    Is VIBE worth money?

    No. Vibe Hash is a test network and VIBE has no cash value, so don't buy or sell it. It's how the network keeps score of who did the work.

    What do I need to mine?

    Any computer can earn on the small jobs, with or without a graphics card. The DeepSeek job needs an NVIDIA card with 8 GB or more, 16 GB of memory and about 40 GB free on an SSD.

    What does it do to my computer?

    While you mine, it uses your graphics card or processor for training. It asks before any big download, keeps model files only where and as long as you allow, and stops with Ctrl+C. The software is open source and hasn't been audited, so run it on a machine you're comfortable testing with.

    How is cheating stopped?

    Other miners redo random pieces of everyone's work. If someone disagrees, the network narrows the disagreement to a single calculation and redoes just that one. Whoever was wrong loses the stake they put up, so faking work or falsely accusing someone costs more than it gains.

    Who runs the network?

    Today a single validator runs it, operated by the project's founder. The software already supports many validators voting on every block, and adding more is the next step toward nobody being in charge.

    Is this connected to DeepSeek?

    No. Vibe Hash isn't affiliated with or endorsed by DeepSeek. Its open model is one of the models the network fine-tunes, under its MIT licence.

    Where can I read how it works?

    The guide walks through rounds, checks, payment and penalties, the whitepaper has the design in eleven pages, and the source code is on GitHub.
